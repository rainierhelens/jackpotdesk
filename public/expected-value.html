Should I play this week?

JackpotDesk prices this Powerball or Mega Millions drawing before you buy. Hit odds do not change: Powerball is 1 in 292,201,338; Mega Millions is 1 in 290,472,336. The question is whether the cash jackpot, after tax and split risk, is worth the $2 or $5 stake.

Use cash, not the TV number

The advertised jackpot is an annuity. The desk uses the cash (lump-sum) value, then applies federal and state tax. That after-tax cash is what a sole jackpot winner actually takes home, before you account for other tickets matching the same numbers.

Split risk is part of the price

Tickets sold is an estimate you can edit. Crowded public tickets (birthdays, 1-2-3-4-5) share a jackpot more often. A unique random ticket has the same hit chance and a slightly better expected payout if you hit, because fewer people hold that combination.

Most weeks, sit it out

After the ticket cost, expected value is usually negative. The board says when you are paying for a story, when it is only entertainment, and when the sketch turns non-negative.
